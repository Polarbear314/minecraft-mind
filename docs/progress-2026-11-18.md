# MindBot: progress and where it's headed

*2026-11-18. A summary for the project's readers (the course, the professor, and whoever picks the work up next). The details are in the design and results docs linked throughout.*

## In one paragraph

MindBot is a creature in a small Minecraft world whose mind is a **Brooks-style subsumption stack**: hand-written survival at the floor, small learned classifiers above it, and small open language models (2–4B) at the top. The project asks **how its behaviour changes from day to day, and why**, with sleep ("dreaming") as the only time it learns. A second question came from an MIT CSAIL report: **can a frontier model (Opus) directly edit another model's weights?**
- **The creature now lives well.** No deaths over the last ~7 Minecraft days, it stays fed, and it harvests, hunts and bakes bread.
- **Nightly learning runs every dawn,** but no learned layer has yet beaten its hand-written version.
- **On weight editing,** Opus edits the small readable classifiers directly every other night. In a sandbox trial this week it also performed a neuron-level edit on the 1.7B Gather model by itself. The edit fixed the targeted flaw on unseen situations far better than random edits, but at a cost the hand-written wiring has to cover.

## The questions

1. **Behaviour across days:** what changes between day 1 and day 100, and which layer's learning caused it? This is measured per Minecraft day from the tick logs.
2. **Editing vs learning:** can Opus improve a layer by writing its weights directly (route A), as well as by writing corrections it is then retrained on (route B)? And how far does that reach, from a 120-number classifier up to a 1.7-billion-parameter model?
3. **Philosophy of mind:**
   - Is a mind still learning *itself* when another mind writes its connections?
   - What does it take for one mind to understand another well enough to change it?
   - Where does the editor end and the edited mind begin?

## What MindBot is now

| Layer (first that speaks wins) | What it is | Learns |
|---|---|---|
| Survive | hand-written: flee, avoid lava and drops, eat to heal | never (the floor) |
| Make | Qwen3-4B (in shadow) | planned: retraining (stage 7) and editing (7b) |
| Shelter | Qwen2.5-3B (in shadow) | planned |
| Gather | Qwen3-1.7B (in shadow): asked only by day with no hostile within 10 blocks, and told what lies in reach | planned |
| Forage | readable classifier (≈120 named weights) | every night: route A (Opus edits) / route B (retrain on Opus's corrections), alternating |
| Wander | readable classifier | same |

Around the stack:
- **Narrator:** Claude Sonnet narrates the creature's thoughts but never decides (a Gazzaniga-style interpreter, and how often its story matches the real cause is logged).
- **Memory:** a weighted memory with embedding retrieval (FreeLAB-style) and forgetting into gist notes.
- **Dreams:** Opus, MindBot's "hippocampus", writes its dreams, plans and nightly reviews.

Day-time decisions use no Claude at all.

## What has been built (stages 1–6, 2026-11-17)

- **Stage 1, behaviour stack:** six layers, hand-written first, with Brooks's inhibition wires (Survive or night-time Shelter pauses Make).
- **Stage 2, weighted memory:** weights on every note, relevance-first retrieval, forgetting with gists and an archive (never deletes).
- **Stage 3, day metrics:** `npm run days` cuts the logs into Minecraft days and measures distance, food, deaths, harvesting, time sheltered and who was in control.
- **Stage 4, learned bottom layers:** readable Forage/Wander classifiers, served only if they beat the hand-written layer on the held-out day (the gate).
- **Stage 5, nightly learning:** at every dawn Opus reviews the day's key moments, writes diagnoses and corrections, the layers are refitted, and the gate decides. **5b:** on alternate nights Opus edits the full weight tables directly, checks each edit against retrieved past moments, then keeps, softens or withdraws it.
- **Stage 6, language-model layers:** Make/Shelter/Gather on Qwen 4B/3B/1.7B in one local service (6.5 GB on the RTX 3060), choosing by letter probabilities from one forward pass, logged in shadow beside the hand-written layers.
- **Fixes from watching it live:**
  - food (crops it couldn't see, plans that starved it);
  - eat-to-heal (a gap Opus found in its first night review);
  - bread;
  - position bias in the small models (each question asked twice with shuffled options).
- **Tests:** `npm run sim` has 325 checks. One older check ("skill feeds back into the decision prompt") is flaky.

## What we've learned so far

**The creature's life** (`results-days.md`). Since the food fixes: no deaths over about 7 Minecraft days, average food 12–17, and it eats, harvests, hunts and makes bread. Nights are spent fully enclosed. One drowning-like death led to a dream note, "Water Above Me Is Not A Roof". Each day Shelter holds the body about half the time, the rest split among Make, Forage, Gather and Wander.

**Learning the bottom layers** (`results-layers.md`). Neither Forage nor Wander has passed its gate yet (latest: Forage 0.55 vs 0.67 for the hand-written layer, Wander 0.29 vs 0.67). The hand-written layers make few mistakes the labels can see, so the learned ones need more good days to learn from.

**Opus editing readable weights (5b).** Opus behaves like a careful editor. On its first night it withdrew an edit that would have changed 282 past moments, and softened one after seeing it would change 1,022 ("the body nearly always carries some food").

**Opus editing a language model: the stage 7b sandbox trial** (`results-7b-trial.md`). This was run on a separate copy of the Gather model, never the live bot. The flaw: Gather says "nothing" by day with something in reach to pick up.

| What was tried | Held-out test cases right (of 20) | Held-out controls right (of 20) |
|---|---|---|
| Unedited | 7 | 15 |
| Part 1: steering, one direction at layer 18, chosen by Opus through a tool | 7 | 13 |
| Prompt change: tell it what lies in reach (no edit) | 11 | 12 |
| **Part 2: Opus's own neuron-level edit (3 groups, about 0.16% of weights)** | **17** | **8** |
| Random edits of the same shape (5 draws) | 6 each | 9–15 |

- **Steering failed.** The model *represents* "worth gathering" clearly, but that knowledge barely drives its answer. Opus saw this and withdrew its edit.
- **Prompt and wiring did the most for the least.** One sentence about what lies in reach nearly doubled correct answers. Telling the model about danger didn't help: it doesn't use "night" or "hostile near" when choosing. So the "when" is now wired into the bot as a rule, and the model only chooses "what".
- **Opus did the operation itself.** It got a map of all 172,032 neurons (fingerprinted on the creature's own situations, grouped, each group's effect measured by switching it off) and a scalpel of ops it writes on named weights. It found groups that fire only in safe daylight yet push toward "nothing", switched them off, and the fix held on unseen situations where random edits did nothing. But it couldn't make the model *hold back* when danger is near: the edit also made it gather at night. **It could remove a learned habit, not install a judgement.**
- **This matches the published work.** The MIT CSAIL paper is now public ("Sparks of Weight-Space Programming", Nov 2026). An independent byte-level check found its headline edit was a single rank-1 change removing one tendency (looping). Several follow-ups (WeightBench, a backdoor study, and Claude Opus 5.5 editing Qwen) appeared within two weeks.
- **What the FreeLAB-style index gave:** a way to navigate a model far too big to read. Opus could never read the 1.7B's weights (billions of tokens' worth, with no meaning in single numbers). The causal measurements (switch a group off and see what changes) did the real work, not similarity of meaning.

## Where it's headed

**Next session:**
1. **Restart the bot** (`windows\start-all.bat`). This also turns on the new Gather rule and prompt.
2. **Edit + rule, in the sandbox (15 minutes):** score Opus's neuron edit together with the live Gather rule. If the rule removes the side effects and keeps the gain, run it in shadow on the live bot for a day or two. That's a weight edit and Brooks-style wiring working together.
3. **Morning check:** `npm run days -- --layers`, the Lab's Night Reviews, and the shadow agreement of the model layers.

**This week:**
4. **A second editing target that is a tendency,** e.g. Shelter staying put too long at dusk. Run the same neuron loop plus a "Sparks-style" arm (one direction, strength by trial) and random edits. Two flaws would support the claim "tendencies are editable, judgement isn't".
5. **Outcome labels and a gate for Make/Shelter/Gather,** so a retrained or edited language model can ever be served on the bot's own results.
6. **More baseline days of the fixed bot,** so the learned Forage/Wander get the examples they need to pass their gates. This is the core result: measured change across days.

**Later, and needing decisions from you:**
7. **Stage 7, retraining the upper layers** while it sleeps. This needs Unsloth/PEFT (a download) and your check of Anthropic's terms, since the layers would learn from Opus's corrections.
8. **A local editor (toward self-editing):** hand route A from Opus to MindBot's own 4B model, compare its edits with Opus's, and later train the editor on which of its edits passed the gate. This turns "where does the editor end and the mind begin?" into an experiment.
9. **Possible fix:** Shelter shouldn't seal in under water.

**Paused on purpose:** deeper weight-editing research (sparse autoencoders, many more rounds). It's a research project in itself, and the cheaper levers (prompt, wiring) gave most of the behaviour gain.
